Enterprise RAG assistant for insurance support

CGI · 2023 – 2024 · Architect

About 90% of questions resolved without a person

An LLM-powered assistant that answers routine insurance questions on its own.

Context

Insurance support teams spend much of their day on routine questions about policies and claims. The goal was an assistant that answers them from the insurer’s own knowledge base, in several languages, around the clock.

What I did

I architected and shipped the system: retrieval-augmented generation over insurance knowledge bases, built with LangChain across Azure, AWS and GCP.

RAG: Retrieval-augmented generation. The model answers from passages retrieved from a knowledge base, not from its training alone.

LangChain: An open-source framework for building applications on language models.

Outcome

About 90% of questions resolved without a person, across a user base of 50,000+. 525% return on investment within 12 months. CGI Impact Award 2024.

Evidence
  • CGI product page: Multi-language AI-driven chatbot for insurance
  • CGI article: AI chatbots for insurance are transforming customer service

Both pages describe the product. They don’t name contributors or publish these figures.

Stack

Python, LangChain, LLMs, RAG, Azure, AWS, GCP

Agent for a proprietary programming language

CGI AI Center of Excellence · 2025 – present · Lead

Projected 50% less manual work for the support team

An agent that reads legacy code in a proprietary language, documents it, and extracts the business rules inside.

Context

A client’s business rules live in legacy scripts written in a proprietary programming language. The scripts need maintaining and documenting now, and migrating later. Because the language is proprietary, general-purpose models can’t be trusted with it out of the box.

What I did

I lead the agent’s design and delivery. A LangGraph dual-state, dynamic workflow coordinates vLLM-served models with retrieval. EBNF grammars constrain outputs so they are always well-formed, and PII filtering keeps personal information out. The agent writes documentation, extracts business rules and supports migration readiness.

LangGraph: An open-source library for building agents as graphs of steps that share state.

vLLM: An open-source engine for serving large language models.

EBNF: Extended Backus–Naur form, a notation for grammars. A model constrained by a grammar can only produce output the grammar allows.

PII: Personally identifiable information: data that identifies a person, such as a name or an email address.

Outcome

Projected 50% reduction in manual work for the support team. In progress.

Evidence

Client work, no public page.

Stack

Python, LangGraph, vLLM, RAG, EBNF structured outputs, PII filtering

Lineage and rule automation with LLMs

80% less manual work on AxiomSL rule updates CGI · 2023 – 2024 · Data architect

Two tools that put language models to work on data operations: tracing incidents and updating rules.

Context

When a report breaks, someone has to trace the data back through several systems by hand. At RBC, AxiomSL rules were also updated by hand.

What I did

I built an OpenLineage-based lineage tool that documents multi-source data flows automatically and uses an LLM to suggest likely root causes. I also automated AxiomSL rule updates at RBC with an LLM, keeping governance controls in place.

Outcome

80% less manual work on AxiomSL rule updates. Shorter investigations across about three incidents a month.

Client work, no public page.

Python, OpenLineage, LLMs, AxiomSL

Computer vision for hockey broadcast video

mAP ≥ 70% on event detection, across 18 hours of game video Elder Laboratory, York University · 2020 – 2021 · Research assistant

Models that watch a hockey broadcast and work out what is happening.

Context

AttentiveVision, a startup co-founded by York professor James Elder, set out to give amateur hockey a broadcast-style viewing experience by following the play automatically.

What I did

I trained a CNN-LSTM stack for event detection on broadcast video, built real-time player tracking with a Kalman filter and OpenPose, and ran an active-learning annotation loop for goals, timeouts and penalties.

Outcome

mAP ≥ 70% on event detection across 18 hours of game video. Tracking under 100 ms at 30 FPS. Labels reached Cohen’s kappa ≥ 0.92.

Evidence
  • Vector Institute: Amateur hockey given professional viewing experience courtesy of machine vision startup co-founded by Vector researcher (March 7, 2022)

The article describes the project. It doesn’t name individual contributors.

Stack

Python, PyTorch, TensorFlow, OpenCV, OpenPose, CNN-LSTM, Kalman filter
